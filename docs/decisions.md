# Decisions

What we changed from the v1 spec (`docs/archive/questline-spec-v1.md`) and why. Newest first. Add a row when you change a decision.

| # | Decision | Replaces (v1) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D30 | The three games are paced to fit a 2.5 minute demo: their timed phases add up to 150 s (Tall tale 7 s to think, 18 s per story, 7 s to call; First impressions 2 rounds of 8 s + 12 s; Left for you 2 questions of 12 s + 3 s, then 30 s to leave one). Each deck is cut to its strongest few: 5 fake and 4 true story sparks, 7 impressions questions, 4 starter trivia questions | Tall tale ran up to 165 s, First impressions 5 rounds (160 s), Left for you 3 questions and 90 s to leave one | Kenny asked for it: the whole demo has to fit in 2.5 minutes with only the best questions. Start screens and end-screen votes are not timed, so a slow pair can still run over |
| D29 | The buttons on a game's last screen (Next game, Again, Finish) are votes: the room moves only when both players picked the same, and each screen shows a tick on your pick and who you are waiting for (`RoomView.votes`). Finish still happens by itself 20 s after the last game | Either player's Next game or Again moved both | One player kept looping the other into the next game. Going on should be something both people want |
| D28 | The playlist ends: after Left for you the only button is Finish (or it finishes by itself 20 s later), and both devices show an end screen; nothing restarts a finished room. Under `GAMES_ONLY`, finishing ends the pair: both riders get a stamp (station = the next stop), the meeting kiosk celebrates in the pair color, and both sit out matching for 90 s. The ride HUD on the glasses (`/g/?ride`) moves the rider's seat onto the lens by itself when the match carries a game, showing the match card until the partner opens the game. A second scripted demo trip, `DEMO_LINE = "Green-B"` (Blandford St to Boston College, above ground), sits beside the Green-D stage trip | Games looped forever ("Next game" after the last goes back to the first), so nothing ended a games-only pair and the kiosk stayed busy; the glasses needed input to get from the match card at `/g/?ride` to the games at `/g/`; the only demo trip was underground on the D | Filming on a real train needs an ending (stamp + LEDs), a glasses wearer with no Neural Band has to end up in the game, and a trip on the surface keeps signal and shows the stops riders see out the window. The games stamp is stored as a mission session with template `games-playlist` so passport queries and the stamps foreign key are unchanged |
| D27 | A trivia question a matched pair leaves in "Left for you" is saved with their two first names and the next pair sees "Left for you by Maya and Sam". For the demo, every question with no names (the starters, and ones left from a room opened by code) shows "Left for you by Andres and Kenny". First impressions shows each player only their partner's guess about them, the deck dropped its yes/no and unanswerable questions, and cats or dogs is a pick of two | The shared list held only the question and its answers, nothing about who left it; both guesses were shown side by side | Kenny asked for it: a left question should say who it is from, and the compare screen and several questions confused players. First names only, the same as the kiosk shows |
| D26 | For the demo, a match leads only to the three two-player games (`GAMES_ONLY = "true"` in the worker's `wrangler.toml`). No kiosk mission starts, the match screen's main button is "Play a game together" with "Play on my glasses" under it, and riders' stops no longer decide who is in the matching pool or when a pair ends. Mission, stamp and stop code stays in place, unused; `GAMES_ONLY = "false"` brings it back | A mission from the deck starts 20 s after a match, picked to fit the time before the next stop, and a rider is checked out at their stop | Kenny asked for it: the demo is the three games played in order, whatever stops the two riders picked. The mission cards looked like extra games, and a stop arriving mid-demo ended the pair |
| D25 | The station catalog is every MBTA subway, light-rail and Mattapan station (124), generated from the MBTA V3 API into `packages/t-map`, and the Passport shows the whole network as a real T map (after Kvrivishvili's 2013 MBTA diagram, CC BY 2.0) with a zoomable full-screen view | A hand-drawn sketch with 15 stations and "…" for the rest of each line | Riders collect stamps across the whole T, so every station needs a place to be stamped and seen; one generated catalog keeps the map, the passport and the progress counts in step with the real network |
| D24 | Linking glasses is not a sign-up step. Sign-up ends on the Ride Mode rules, which create the rider; riders with glasses link them from Me > Glasses | B7 "Link your glasses" as the last onboarding step, with "No glasses" to skip | Most riders have no glasses, so the step was a screen to skip for nearly everyone; the phone shows everything the glasses would |
| D23 | In the trivia game "Left for you", each pair leaves one question about the T or Boston for the next pair. The pair writes the question and the right answer; the model writes the three wrong answers and can turn the question down (off topic, offensive, personal). A curated starter deck fills in when few are left. Missions are still never generated | The LLM only writes the "why you two" line | Kenny asked for it: playing what the last pair left is the hook. The model never writes the fact, only the decoys, and nothing is saved without its check |
| D24 | A player in a two-player game can move their seat from their phone to a pair of glasses and back ("Play on glasses" at `/m`). The glasses show their usual 4-digit code at `/g/`; the player types it on the phone. | Both players arrive by tapping an NFC tag with a phone, so pairing happens phone to phone, and only one of them has glasses. The glasses cannot type, so the phone enters the code. Only where the seat is drawn changes: the game stays in its `GameRoom`, so scores, timers and turns carry over, and the phone takes over again for the one screen that needs typing. |
| D25 | When two riders are matched, the worker opens a game room for the pair and sends its code and each rider's seat in the match (`MatchInfo.game`). The phone's match and mission screens get a "Play a game together" button that opens `/m` in that room. | The tap is how riders arrive, so the games should start from the match, with no code to read out and type. The kiosk mission is unchanged; the games sit alongside it. |
| D22 | Two-player games run in a `GameRoom` Durable Object keyed by a 4-digit code: glasses host at `/g/`, a phone joins at `/m`. Pure game modules in the protocol package, clients poll, no login | Single-device games on the glasses with no server | The demo is two people, one on glasses and one on a phone, and their screens must agree. Polling because WebSockets are unconfirmed on the glasses. The code is the only key, which is fine for a demo and should become the match once this joins the ride flow |
| D21 | Two ways in on the landing page: a one-time email code (D20) and "Just play today" with a first name. No Sign in with Apple. After BEGIN the start screen's train doors close, then open onto the landing page (or Ride Mode), whose pieces rise in as they part | Apple, email link and guest buttons; the start screen cut straight to the landing page | Fewer choices on the first screen, and no Apple developer setup for a hackathon. The doors make the start screen and the app one continuous motion instead of a hard cut |
| D20 | Email accounts with a 6-digit one-time code, sent with Cloudflare Email Sending (`send_email` binding). New emails onboard and get an account; known emails sign straight back in. "Just play today" stays as an email-less guest. A signed-out kiosk tap goes through sign-in and then checks in | Device-only riders (token in `localStorage`, lost with the browser) | Riders keep their passport across phones, and the kiosk tap works for someone who isn't signed in yet. Codes beat magic links on a train: no switching apps mid-tap, and iOS offers the code from Mail |
| D19 | The ride screens with a train in them show a live 3D platform view from the start screen's renderer: on Riding (C2) trolleys keep pulling in beside your rider; on Ride Mode off (C4/C5) one pulls in at your stop and stays | Flat CSS scenes: a line strip with a rider sprite, a tunnel with a sliding train | Seeing the train come in is the moment the app is about; one renderer keeps it in the same look. The flat scenes stay as the fallback without WebGL |
| D18 | The start screen's island is a small Boston University: the stacked Center for Computing & Data Sciences building in the middle, Warren Towers, Marsh Chapel and Bay State Road brownstones, with green and autumn trees | A generic town (clock tower, rowhouses, pond) | The hackathon is at BU; the island should be a place the judges and riders recognise |
| D17 | Every phone screen follows the start screen: block-letter headings (letter tiles for section names), nothing tilted, and all artwork rendered by the start screen's own renderer into `apps/phone-web/public/art` (`pnpm art`). Only pictures the app uses are rendered (landing island, rider, glasses, and sprites for the flat fallback scenes) | Hand-lettered headings, tilted buttons and cards, CSS-drawn scenes, empty art placeholders | One look across the app; art that can be re-rendered when a colour or model changes instead of redrawn by hand |
| D16 | The game is called **In This Town**. The T of TOWN is a green roundel, for the T (the MBTA). A 3D start screen (`packages/start-screen`) opens the phone app once per visit. Players see the new name; code identifiers, the database and `questline.page` keep "questline" | Questline; the app opened straight on the landing page | The name is about Boston and its train, and the T carries the nod. Renaming code identifiers and the domain would be churn for nothing a player sees |
| D15 | Ride Mode is confirmed on every ride. Onboarding ends with a rules step (18+ and "Ride Mode on means I can be matched"). The first tap of each ride shows your stop, then a "Turn on Ride Mode?" screen, and only its button sends `rideMode: true` with the check-in. Turning it off and "Not now" each have their own confirm | A tap alone turned Ride Mode on | Since there is no accept step per match, the one yes has to be clear and on purpose. A tag tap can be accidental |
| D14 | One vocabulary, the phone's: its 34 interest ids are the protocol's `InterestTag`, social battery is `nod/chat/talk`, status `local/student/visiting`, and stops carry ids that match the phone's station catalog. The demo trip moved to the Green Line D branch (Kenmore to Government Ctr) to match the phone's station art | 25 worker tags folded from the phone's list; stop names only; Orange Line demo | Kenny's phone design and the worker were built from different specs. One source of truth beats an adapter that pads interests and guesses station ids |
| D13 | Glasses work with zero input. Arrival = tapping the meeting kiosk's tag, missions auto-start, "Not now" is on the phone. Temple touch strip is a bonus | Neural Band pinch for "I'm here" / "Not now" | We don't have the Neural Band. The touch strip sends arrow keys, but which gesture sends Enter / Back isn't documented |
| D12 | Host everything on one Cloudflare Worker: static assets for the 3 web apps, Hono API, D1, one `TrainRoom` Durable Object per trip | Separate backend | One deploy, one HTTPS origin (needed for the glasses Web App and NFC links), DOs are a natural fit for per-train live state |
| D11 | No native app. Phone is a web app, glasses are a Meta Ray-Ban Display **Web App** | iOS app + Device Access Toolkit | We dropped camera and voice, so the Web App covers everything we need. Cuts Swift, Xcode and Meta SDK setup. Tradeoff: the glasses page must stay open during the ride (no notifications) |
| D10 | Glasses link via a 4-digit code shown on the lens and typed on the phone | n/a | Glasses Web Apps have no text input |
| D9 | Location comes from tapping the kiosk NFC tag (check-in to a car). No BLE beacons, no GPS | BLE badge RSSI + station beacons | iPhones read NFC tags in the background and open the link. No location permission, no background BLE limits, works underground |
| D8 | Stamp proof: both riders tap the tag with the current nonce, which the Pi rewrites per mission | Badge-to-badge BLE/NFC handshake | iPhones can't act as an NFC card, but they read tags fine. Fresh nonce stops photo replay |
| D7 | A kiosk per train car (Pi + touchscreen + LED strip + dynamic NFC tag) | Wearable badge, camera lock-on, AprilTag | Fixed meeting point solves "which person is it", gives a shared screen for the mission, and is real hardware to demo |
| D6 | Matching: same trip + overlapping remaining stops, interest gate, then closest car wins | Mutual top-match, interest-ranked, platform first | "Closest person who shares interests" is simpler and works on a moving train |
| D5 | Ride Mode on = consent. No accept step. One pinch for "Not now", ghost mode always available | Both say yes per mission | Fewer steps. "Not now" covers the "not this person right now" case |
| D4 | On-train play is primary. Platform play is out of scope for now | Platform primary | The kiosk lives in the car |
| D3 | No camera, no voice, no AR anchoring | Lock-on reticle, voice yes/skip | The display is a fixed HUD, not world-anchored AR. Camera and mic aren't available to Web Apps anyway |
| D2 | No UWB arrows | Directional arrows to your match | UWB direction is unreliable on newer iPhones and needs foreground + a forward-facing device. The kiosk meeting point makes it unnecessary |
| D1 | Missions still come from a curated deck, still fit before the next stop minus 30 s | (unchanged) | Safety and latency |
